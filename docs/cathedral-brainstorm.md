# Cathedral of Pain: episode brainstorm

A summary of one brainstorming session (2026-11-17) on what locations and arc the Cathedral of Pain episode (`wads/CATHEDRAL.WAD`) could have. Nothing in it is planned or built yet. **Decisions** are tmoney's and are marked as such; everything else is an open thread or a question. Only Cursed Altar (`cursed_altar`, now MAP01) exists.

## Where it started

Cursed Altar gave the episode two things to work with. One is its shape: you walk in, take something, the space behind you changes, and you walk back out (the red skull sinks the nave's pillars). The other is a question the map never answers: what did taking the skull do?

Five episode shapes were pitched: one building, going down; the cathedral precinct seen from outside; Cursed Altar's recessional at episode scale (in, take, back out through the same places changed); the hours of a liturgical day instead of places; the building as a body in pain. tmoney picked **going down** and **the recessional**, which combine into: down through the layers, then back up through them, changed.

## The arc

**Decided:**

- **Five maps down, MAP01-05.** Not a symmetric 4 down, 4 up ("no way"). The first take was 5 in and 3 up; it was then condensed.
- **MAP06 and MAP07 are the way up**, the maps that bring back landmarks from the way down.
- **MAP07 is the largest, most complex and hardest map**, with the most landmark recall.
- **MAP08 is paused.** tmoney's current pull: "you finally climb out of the rubble of the cathedral and there is a big boss fight waiting for you."
- **Cursed Altar stays**, but not necessarily as MAP01: "MAP01 is just a test map that created this idea. It doesn't have to be the first map. It's good enough that I want to keep it though, so it has to fit in."

## Coming back changed: recognizable but not the same

tmoney's concern, which shaped everything after it: "I'm worried about the lack of expressiveness of the engine. If we change the textures on the way out to mirror the bottom, then does it still feel like the way out? The tension I see is between the player recognizing 'oh this is the same map as before' but it being too similar."

Retexturing alone probably reads as a reskin ("same map, darker"). DOOM expresses more with height, light, facing and what you can walk on, and a return map is a separate map, so nothing ties it to the old layout. Four ways to split "what to keep so you recognize it, what to break so it plays differently" were offered (keep the landmarks and change the routes; height as the change; going behind the walls you saw; sameness as bait). tmoney chose **keep the landmarks, change the routes**.

**Decided:**

- **Landmarks are places where something happened.** The first half's set pieces become the second half's landmarks: the fountain where you took the key, the altar where you took the skull. Recognition comes from silhouette at DOOM speed, and it brings back what happened there, not just the shape.
- **You arrive from an angle you never saw**, the trap's other side being the strongest example: on the way back you come out of the closet the hell knight came out of, onto the cloister walk, with the fountain in front of you. Sides to vary between maps: from underneath, from behind, from above, from inside. One side every time would be a formula by the third map.
- **Skull switches turn walls into doors** onto the landmark. You're somewhere new, press a skull switch, the wall drops, and behind it is a room you know. It echoes Cursed Altar, where the red skull changed the nave.
- **"Movie magic."** The way down does not hint at the re-entrances, and they aren't mapped on the way down. The layout doesn't have to make spatial sense: "You go through some twisty corridors and you're at the fountain. Is it where the fountain would have logically been? Who cares!" A true spire or shaft doesn't matter either.
- **The old arena becomes a quiet passage.** Each landmark comes back nearly empty, and the real fight is somewhere next to it: "the entire return map doesn't have to be fought in the same area as the initial, just walk past the altar on the way to the real fight the second time." tmoney: "Love making the landmarks quiet sections on the way up." The hunch behind it: a quiet walk-through is where recall lands hardest, because nothing competes for attention.
- **Only the strongest landmarks return, picked after playing MAP01-05.** The down maps are built without promising any returns; which landmarks stuck is decided from memory after playtesting. Consequence: the down maps have no hidden backs, since nobody knew which walls would open later. Several landmarks from one down map may return.
- **MAP07 recalls through big set pieces**, "not a slightly different altar".

**Still open:**

- **How a set piece comes back in MAP07.** Three modes were pitched: a quiet walk through the aftermath; the same trap turned around next door (for example, you are the one sealed in a sinking pillar); or a bigger version of it.
- **Memory fades across the episode.** MAP02's landmarks may be played nights before MAP07, so the earliest maps need the loudest landmarks. Cursed Altar's axis view (the great doors opening on the whole nave and the altar 3000 units off) is the one everyone will remember. From above, from the clerestory, it would show the sunk pillars and the funnel stair.
- **MAP07 as a cross-section.** You climb past the landmarks of the down maps, each seen from its broken side. With movie magic it needn't be a real shaft, just "twisty corridors and you're at the fountain". Risk: a museum of references, one landmark per landing. The landmarks would have to be part of the route, not windows you pass.

## The way down: vocabulary and aesthetic

**Decided:**

- **The room vocabulary is church, altar, graveyard, crypt.** Any of them can recur across maps ("multiple maps can have graveyards! Multiple maps can have crypts!"), but they are not duplicated into every map: "I meant that the different room types I mentioned COULD be reused, not that they should be duplicated across every map!"
- **No real-building logic.** The descent doesn't have to follow what a real cathedral has under it (undercroft, then catacombs, then cistern). tmoney: "I think if you keep the 'church-altar-graveyard-crypt' map areas, and nail the 'going down' aesthetic, everything else will fall out and re-visiting the landmarks will be the real payoff."
- **The sky is the descent hint**: "I like 1-3 with sky and 3-6 without as a good descent hint." The red hell sky (`SKY3`, set in UMAPINFO as in Cursed Altar) over MAP01-03; MAP03 is where it closes; none in MAP03-06.

**Live threads:**

- **Older as you go down.** Each layer is older and rougher than the one above. Materials: marble, then grey stone, brick, raw rock, and flesh and bone at the bottom; DOOM II's textures already run in that order. Altars: a marble pedestal, a stone block, a carved boulder, bare rock with something on it. Graveyards: headstones, slabs, cairns, pits. The light sources may carry it best: candelabras, then torches, braziers, and finally lava. Old age has to show in shapes and light, since DOOM II has no textures for different eras: heavier walls, rounder arches, rougher stone.
- **MAP03, where the sky closes.** For example, a churchyard collapsed into a sinkhole, with the last look at the sky from the bottom of it. Losing the sky is the loudest "we're below now" signal, and the graveyard, the most outdoor of the four rooms, can carry it: open under the sky near the top, then in a cavern under a rock ceiling with one shaft of red light from far up, then no sky at all. Tension: low ceilings cramp big fights, so the deepest maps may need huge caverns.
- **MAP01 as an approach from outside, with Cursed Altar at MAP02.** A baron and two hell knights make Cursed Altar a steep opener next to DOOM II's MAP01 (zombiemen and imps). A shorter, gentler surface map approaching the cathedral (through a graveyard, say) would put Cursed Altar at MAP02, where its difficulty fits. The cathedral would need to stay in view for an approach map to work. Cursed Altar is a natural top-of-descent map either way: marble, sky over the garth, graveyard and porch, the full cathedral, and its crypt as the first step down. Open: does the episode open inside the cathedral or outside it?

## MAP05: the bottom and the cyberdemon crusher

This answers why you turn back at the bottom: you open the wrong door.

**How it got here**, three steps from tmoney:

1. A red key at the bottom of MAP05 opens a room with about eight cyberdemons, but you already saw the red door and can just exit. Does that break players who want 100% kills?
2. "Or maybe the CDs come out of closets into a room that you already know is a crusher, and you have to run back and quickly crush them?"
3. "Red key, door on the immediate right, you loop around to the back and crush, exit in the middle."

On step 1: some players play every map for 100% kills (UV-Max is an established demo category). Leaving eight counted cyberdemons ends the tally around 80-something percent, and with rockets alone killing them is effectively impossible (numbers below). Step 2 turns knowing the map into the weapon, the episode's recall idea at the scale of one map, and since crusher kills count toward the player's total, the crusher is the route to 100%.

**Decided layout:**

```
        +--------------------------------------------+
        | loop corridor        [crusher switch]  <   |
        +-----+---w------w------w------w-----+       |
 closets      |                              |   ^   |
 [CD][CD] --> |                              w       |
 [CD][CD] --> |         crusher room         |   ^   |
 [CD][CD] --> |            (exit)            w       |
 [CD][CD] --> |                              |   ^   |
              +------------------------------+       |
              | red key             red door =   ^   |
              +------------------------------+-------+

 w = window into the crusher room; arrows = the run from the red door to the switch
```

- **Taking the red key** opens closets that release the cyberdemons into the crusher room.
- **The red door is immediately to the right of the key.** Through it a corridor loops round the crusher room to the crusher's switch at the back.
- **Windows along the loop** into the crusher room: a rocket gauntlet at each one. (Chosen over a solid wall with one window at the switch, a solid wall, or bars the whole way.)
- **The exit is in the middle of the crusher room.** It is behind the cyberdemons, so the crush can't be skipped, and everyone who finishes the map gets 100% kills.

**How it plays** (from vanilla behaviour; not yet tested in the engine):

- **They follow you round the loop.** Once awake, DOOM monsters walk toward their target whether or not they can see it; they need sight only to attack. As you run the loop they track you along the inside of the wall, so by the time you reach the switch they're piled against the back wall, under the crusher. The crusher may only need to cover that back strip, leaving the middle, with the exit, as a normal floor.
- **The windows cut both ways.** A monster stops walking while it attacks, so cyberdemons firing through each window may lag behind you on the way round. This wasn't explored further.
- **You hear them the whole way.** DOOM doesn't muffle sound through walls, and cyberdemons stomp when they walk: eight of them keeping pace just behind the wall.
- **The crusher room can have no ordinary door.** Monsters open plain use doors (DR) and would come through into the loop. Locked doors and switch- or trigger-opened doors hold them.

**Engine numbers** (vanilla source; not yet measured in this project's tools):

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Crusher damage | 10 HP every 4 tics to everything under it at once, about 87 HP/s; eight die as fast as one |
| Cyberdemon health | 4,000: about 46 s under a crusher that never lifts, roughly a minute over two passes |
| Slow vs fast crushers | A slow crusher drops to 1/8 speed when it hits something; a fast one doesn't |
| Caught monsters | Can't walk while the ceiling gap is less than their height (cyberdemon 110), so they stay caught until it lifts |
| Crushed corpses | Turn into gib piles: no cyberdemon bodies afterwards, just red mess |
| Kills by a crusher | Count toward the player's kills in single player |
| Rockets vs cyberdemons | Immune to splash; a direct hit does 20-160 (average 90). Eight need about 350 rockets; the cap with a backpack is 100 |
| Infighting | Cyberdemons don't hurt each other, so they won't thin themselves out |
| Size | 80 units wide; big monsters tend to jam in narrow openings |

**Still open:**

- How many cyberdemons on each skill. "About eight" is spectacle; two or three, lit and shown before the wall opens, were pitched as possibly more frightening ("too big" rather than "too many").
- How you get into the room after the crush: the switch also opens a wall onto the gibs, or a second switch does.
- What the exit in the middle is. A hunch: a lift up from the floor where they died, so MAP05 ends where the way up begins and hands straight to MAP06.
- Crusher speed and coverage, and how long the room takes under fire.
- The rest of MAP05 before this room.
- Whether any cyberdemons come back later. One pitch had the horde waiting for you in MAP08, fought on your terms; it wasn't taken up, and if all of them die in MAP05 it can't happen as stated.

## Considered and dropped

Kept here so they aren't re-pitched.

- **Episode shapes not chosen:** the cathedral precinct seen from outside (the town, the spire as a landmark in every map); the hours of a liturgical day as the arc (light and sky instead of places); the building as a body in pain, which was close to the Icon of Sin anyway.
- **A symmetric 4-down, 4-up mirror:** "no way".
- **A cause or pursuer behind the changes:** "if I'm being pursued (which as you pointed out isn't really possible anyway), why would the walls already be changed when I get there?" Coming at the same large structure from a different angle carries it instead.
- **Retexturing the way up to mirror the bottom** as the main change: reads as "same map, darker".
- **Hinting the re-entrances on the way down:** movie magic instead.
- **Literal cathedral logic** (what a real building has under it, and how deep).
- **Every room type in every map.**
- **Recall by near-identical rooms** (a row of altars at different depths): MAP07 recalls by big set pieces.
- **Others offered and dropped without being taken up:** height as the change on the way back (flooded, drained, fallen floors); going behind the walls you saw, looking in on the old rooms; sameness as bait (a few small things wrong); views into other layers through grates and shafts; the quarry the stone was cut from; the cathedral's machinery (bells, organ bellows, furnaces); an upside-down cathedral at the bottom; room proportions shifting with depth (the crypt growing, the church shrinking); a "what's at the bottom" taxonomy (break it, wake it, take it).
